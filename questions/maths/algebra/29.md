## Which of the following is NOT equal to $y^2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}$?

---

#### Expand each option and compare with $y^2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}$.

**Option $(y^4+1)/y^2$:**
$$\frac{y^4+1}{y^2} = \frac{y^4}{y^2} + \frac{1}{y^2} = y^2 + \frac{1}{y^2}$$
This **is equal** to $y^2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}$.

**Option $\left(y+\dfrac{1}{y}\right)^2$:**

Using $(a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2$ with $a=y, b=\dfrac1y$:
$$\left(y+\frac{1}{y}\right)^2 = y^2 + 2\cdot y \cdot \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{y^2} = y^2 + 2 + \frac{1}{y^2}$$
This is **not equal** to $y^2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}$ — it exceeds it by $2$.

**Option $\left(y-\dfrac{1}{y}\right)^2$:**
$$\left(y-\frac{1}{y}\right)^2 = y^2 - 2 + \frac{1}{y^2}$$
This is also **not equal** to $y^2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}$ — it is short by $2$.

**Option $\left(y+\dfrac{1}{y}\right)^2 - 2$:**
$$\left(y+\frac{1}{y}\right)^2 - 2 = \left(y^2+2+\frac{1}{y^2}\right) - 2 = y^2 + \frac{1}{y^2}$$
This **is equal** to $y^2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}$.

**Conclusion:** Strictly by direct expansion, both $\left(y+\dfrac1y\right)^2$ and $\left(y-\dfrac1y\right)^2$ fail to equal $y^2+\dfrac{1}{y^2}$ (differing by $+2$ and $-2$ respectively), while an MCQ expects exactly one correct choice. The paper's answer key designates $\left(y+\dfrac1y\right)^2$ as the intended answer — it is the "plain square" distractor, the classic trap for students who forget the middle $2ab$ term — so it is taken as the official single answer here.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{\left(y+\frac{1}{y}\right)^2}$$

**Note:** $\left(y-\dfrac1y\right)^2$ is also algebraically unequal to $y^2+\dfrac1{y^2}$ and is listed among the choices (not the marked answer) per the official key.
