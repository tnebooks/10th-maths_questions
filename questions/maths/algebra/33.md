## A bus travels a distance of $90$ km at a uniform speed. If its speed had been increased by $15$ km/hr, the journey would have taken $30$ minutes less. Find the speed of the bus.

---

#### Let the original speed of the bus be $x$ km/hr.

Time taken at the original speed:
$$t_1 = \frac{90}{x} \text{ hours}$$

New speed $= (x+15)$ km/hr. Time taken at the new speed:
$$t_2 = \frac{90}{x+15} \text{ hours}$$

The journey time is reduced by $30$ minutes $= \dfrac{1}{2}$ hour, so:
$$t_1 - t_2 = \frac{1}{2}$$
$$\frac{90}{x} - \frac{90}{x+15} = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Step 1: Combine the left side over a common denominator.**
$$\frac{90(x+15) - 90x}{x(x+15)} = \frac{1}{2}$$
$$\frac{90x + 1350 - 90x}{x(x+15)} = \frac{1}{2}$$
$$\frac{1350}{x(x+15)} = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Step 2: Cross-multiply.**
$$1350 \times 2 = x(x+15)$$
$$2700 = x^2 + 15x$$
$$x^2 + 15x - 2700 = 0$$

**Step 3: Solve the quadratic equation.**

Using the quadratic formula with $a=1, b=15, c=-2700$:
$$\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 15^2 - 4(1)(-2700) = 225 + 10800 = 11025$$
$$\sqrt{11025} = 105$$
$$x = \frac{-15 \pm 105}{2}$$

So $x = \dfrac{90}{2} = 45$ or $x = \dfrac{-120}{2} = -60$.

Since speed cannot be negative, reject $x=-60$.
$$x = 45$$

**Step 4: Verify.**

At $x=45$ km/hr: $t_1 = \dfrac{90}{45} = 2$ hours.
At $x+15=60$ km/hr: $t_2 = \dfrac{90}{60} = 1.5$ hours.
Difference: $2 - 1.5 = 0.5$ hour $= 30$ minutes. ✓ matches the given condition.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{\text{Speed of the bus} = 45 \text{ km/hr}}$$
