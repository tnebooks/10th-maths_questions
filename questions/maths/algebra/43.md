## Find the square root of the polynomial $x^4-12x^3+42x^2-36x+9$ by the division method

---

#### Apply the long-division method for polynomial square roots, working term by term.

**Step 1: First term of the quotient.**

$\sqrt{x^4}=x^2$. Take $x^2$ as the first term of the quotient. Square it and subtract:
$$x^4-12x^3+42x^2-36x+9 - (x^2)^2 = -12x^3+42x^2-36x+9$$

**Step 2: Second term of the quotient.**

Double the quotient so far: $2x^2$. Divide the new leading term $-12x^3$ by $2x^2$:
$$\frac{-12x^3}{2x^2}=-6x$$

New divisor: $2x^2-6x$. Multiply by $-6x$:
$$(2x^2-6x)(-6x) = -12x^3+36x^2$$

Subtract from the remainder:
$$(-12x^3+42x^2-36x+9)-(-12x^3+36x^2) = 6x^2-36x+9$$

**Step 3: Third term of the quotient.**

Double the quotient so far ($x^2-6x$): $2x^2-12x$. Divide the new leading term $6x^2$ by $2x^2$:
$$\frac{6x^2}{2x^2}=3$$

New divisor: $2x^2-12x+3$. Multiply by $3$:
$$(2x^2-12x+3)(3) = 6x^2-36x+9$$

Subtract from the remainder:
$$(6x^2-36x+9)-(6x^2-36x+9)=0$$

The remainder is $0$, so the division terminates exactly. The quotient is the square root:
$$x^2-6x+3$$

**Step 4: Verify by squaring the quotient.**

$$(x^2-6x+3)^2 = x^4+36x^2+9-12x^3+6x^2-36x$$

More carefully, using $(A+B+C)^2=A^2+B^2+C^2+2AB+2AC+2BC$ with $A=x^2,\ B=-6x,\ C=3$:
$$= x^4+36x^2+9+2(x^2)(-6x)+2(x^2)(3)+2(-6x)(3)$$
$$= x^4+36x^2+9-12x^3+6x^2-36x$$
$$= x^4-12x^3+42x^2-36x+9 ✓$$

This matches the original polynomial exactly.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{x^2-6x+3}$$
