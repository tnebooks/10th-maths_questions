## Solve: $x+2y-z=5;\ x-y+z=-2;\ -5x-4y+z=-11$

---

#### Label the equations and eliminate variables systematically.

Let:
$$x+2y-z=5 \quad \text{...(1)}$$
$$x-y+z=-2 \quad \text{...(2)}$$
$$-5x-4y+z=-11 \quad \text{...(3)}$$

**Step 1: Eliminate $z$ using (1) and (2).**

Adding (1) and (2):
$$(x+x)+(2y-y)+(-z+z)=5+(-2)$$
$$2x+y=3 \quad \text{...(4)}$$

**Step 2: Eliminate $z$ using (2) and (3).**

From (2): $z=-2-x+y$. Substitute into (3):
$$-5x-4y+(-2-x+y)=-11$$
$$-6x-3y-2=-11$$
$$-6x-3y=-9$$
$$2x+y=3 \quad \text{...(5)}$$

**Step 3: Compare (4) and (5).**

Equations (4) and (5) are **identical**: $2x+y=3$. This means equation (3) is not independent of (1) and (2) — the three original equations are **linearly dependent**, and the system does not have a unique solution.

**Confirming the dependency directly:** Check whether (3) is a linear combination of (1) and (2), i.e. whether $(3) = a\cdot(1)+b\cdot(2)$ for some constants $a,b$.

Matching coefficients of $x$: $a+b=-5$
Matching coefficients of $y$: $2a-b=-4$
Matching coefficients of $z$: $-a+b=1$

From the first and third equations: $a+b=-5$ and $-a+b=1$. Adding these: $2b=-4 \Rightarrow b=-2$, and then $a=-3$.

Check against the $y$-coefficient equation: $2a-b=2(-3)-(-2)=-6+2=-4$ ✓ (matches).

Check the constant term: $a(5)+b(-2) = -3(5)-2(-2) = -15+4=-11$ ✓ (matches equation (3)'s RHS).

So $(3) = -3\times(1) - 2\times(2)$ exactly — confirming equation (3) carries no new information.

**Step 4: Write the general (parametric) solution.**

Since only two independent equations remain, one variable is free. Let $x=t$ (any real number).

From (4): $y=3-2x=3-2t$

From (2): $z=-2-x+y = -2-t+(3-2t) = 1-3t$

**Step 5: Verify by substituting the parametric solution back into ALL THREE original equations.**

Check (1): $x+2y-z = t+2(3-2t)-(1-3t) = t+6-4t-1+3t = (t-4t+3t)+(6-1) = 0+5=5$ ✓

Check (2): $x-y+z = t-(3-2t)+(1-3t) = t-3+2t+1-3t = (t+2t-3t)+(-3+1) = 0-2=-2$ ✓

Check (3): $-5x-4y+z = -5t-4(3-2t)+(1-3t) = -5t-12+8t+1-3t = (-5t+8t-3t)+(-12+1) = 0-11=-11$ ✓

All three original equations are satisfied for every value of $t$, confirming the system is consistent but **underdetermined** (dependent equations) — it has **infinitely many solutions**.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{x=t,\ y=3-2t,\ z=1-3t \quad \text{for any real } t \text{ (infinitely many solutions)}}$$
