## If the equation $(c^2-ab)x^2-2(a^2-bc)x+(b^2-ac)=0$ has real and equal roots, prove that $a=0$ or $a^3+b^3+c^3=3abc$

---

#### For a quadratic $Ax^2+Bx+C=0$, roots are real and equal when the discriminant $\Delta=B^2-4AC=0$.

Here:
$$A=c^2-ab, \qquad B=-2(a^2-bc), \qquad C=b^2-ac$$

**Step 1: Set the discriminant to zero.**
$$B^2-4AC=0$$
$$[-2(a^2-bc)]^2-4(c^2-ab)(b^2-ac)=0$$
$$4(a^2-bc)^2-4(c^2-ab)(b^2-ac)=0$$

Dividing throughout by $4$:
$$(a^2-bc)^2-(c^2-ab)(b^2-ac)=0$$

**Step 2: Expand $(a^2-bc)^2$.**
$$(a^2-bc)^2 = a^4-2a^2bc+b^2c^2$$

**Step 3: Expand $(c^2-ab)(b^2-ac)$.**
$$(c^2-ab)(b^2-ac) = c^2b^2-ac^3-ab^3+a^2bc$$

**Step 4: Substitute both expansions.**
$$a^4-2a^2bc+b^2c^2 - \left(b^2c^2-ac^3-ab^3+a^2bc\right)=0$$
$$a^4-2a^2bc+b^2c^2-b^2c^2+ac^3+ab^3-a^2bc=0$$
$$a^4-3a^2bc+ac^3+ab^3=0$$

**Step 5: Factor out $a$.**
$$a\left(a^3-3abc+c^3+b^3\right)=0$$
$$a\left(a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc\right)=0$$

**Step 6: Conclude.**

Since the product is zero, at least one factor must be zero:
$$a=0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc=0$$

That is:
$$a=0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad a^3+b^3+c^3=3abc$$

**Verification (numerical check):** Take $a=1,\ b=1,\ c=1$ (so $a^3+b^3+c^3=3=3abc$, satisfying the second condition). Then $A=c^2-ab=1-1=0$, $B=-2(a^2-bc)=-2(1-1)=0$, $C=b^2-ac=1-1=0$ — the "equation" degenerates, consistent with the boundary case. Taking $a=0,\ b=2,\ c=3$ (satisfying $a=0$): $A=c^2-ab=9-0=9$, $B=-2(a^2-bc)=-2(0-6)=12$, $C=b^2-ac=4-0=4$. Discriminant $=B^2-4AC=144-4(9)(4)=144-144=0$ ✓, confirming real and equal roots as required.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{a=0 \ \text{ or } \ a^3+b^3+c^3=3abc \quad \text{(proved)}}$$
