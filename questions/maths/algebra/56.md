## If $A = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \sin\theta \end{pmatrix}$, show that $A^2 + B^2 = I$.

---

#### **Given matrices:**
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \sin\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

**Step 1: Compute $A^2$**
$$A^2 = A \cdot A = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$$
$$A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} (\cos\theta)(\cos\theta) + (0)(0) & (\cos\theta)(0) + (0)(\cos\theta) \\ (0)(\cos\theta) + (\cos\theta)(0) & (0)(0) + (\cos\theta)(\cos\theta) \end{pmatrix}$$
$$A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} \cos^2\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \cos^2\theta \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$

**Step 2: Compute $B^2$**
$$B^2 = B \cdot B = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \sin\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \sin\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \sin\theta \end{pmatrix}$$
$$B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} (\sin\theta)(\sin\theta) + (0)(0) & (\sin\theta)(0) + (0)(\sin\theta) \\ (0)(\sin\theta) + (\sin\theta)(0) & (0)(0) + (\sin\theta)(\sin\theta) \end{pmatrix}$$
$$B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} \sin^2\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \sin^2\theta \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$

**Step 3: Add $A^2$ and $B^2$**
$$A^2 + B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} \cos^2\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \cos^2\theta \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} \sin^2\theta & 0 \\ 0 & \sin^2\theta \end{pmatrix}$$
$$A^2 + B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta & 0 + 0 \\ 0 + 0 & \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

Using the fundamental Pythagorean trigonometric identity $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$:
$$A^2 + B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I$$

Hence, $A^2 + B^2 = I$ is proved.
