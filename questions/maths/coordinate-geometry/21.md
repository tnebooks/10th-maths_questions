## Without using the Pythagoras theorem, show that the points $(1, -4)$, $(2, -3)$ and $(4, -7)$ form a right-angled triangle.

---

#### Let $A = (1, -4)$, $B = (2, -3)$, $C = (4, -7)$. We use the slope method: if the product of the slopes of two sides is $-1$, those two sides are perpendicular, which means the triangle has a right angle between them.

Slope formula:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

Slope of $AB$:
$$m_{AB} = \frac{-3 - (-4)}{2 - 1} = \frac{1}{1} = 1$$

Slope of $BC$:
$$m_{BC} = \frac{-7 - (-3)}{4 - 2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2$$

Slope of $AC$:
$$m_{AC} = \frac{-7 - (-4)}{4 - 1} = \frac{-3}{3} = -1$$

Checking products of slopes for each pair of sides:
$$m_{AB} \times m_{BC} = 1 \times (-2) = -2 \quad (\text{not } -1)$$
$$m_{BC} \times m_{AC} = (-2) \times (-1) = 2 \quad (\text{not } -1)$$
$$m_{AB} \times m_{AC} = 1 \times (-1) = -1 \quad \checkmark$$

Since $m_{AB} \times m_{AC} = -1$, the sides $AB$ and $AC$ are perpendicular to each other. Both sides meet at the common point $A = (1, -4)$.

**Answer:** The triangle has a right angle at $\mathbf{A(1, -4)}$, since $AB \perp AC$. Hence $(1, -4)$, $(2, -3)$ and $(4, -7)$ form a right-angled triangle.
