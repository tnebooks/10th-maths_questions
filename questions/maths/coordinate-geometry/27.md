## Find the equation of the straight line passing through the point of intersection of the lines $5x - 6y = 2$ and $3x + 2y = 10$, and perpendicular to the line $4x - 7y + 13 = 0$.

---

#### Step 1: Find the point of intersection of $5x - 6y = 2$ and $3x + 2y = 10$.

From the second equation:
$$3x + 2y = 10 \implies y = \frac{10 - 3x}{2}$$

Substitute into the first equation:
$$5x - 6\left(\frac{10-3x}{2}\right) = 2$$
$$5x - 3(10 - 3x) = 2$$
$$5x - 30 + 9x = 2$$
$$14x = 32$$
$$x = \frac{32}{14} = \frac{16}{7}$$

Substitute back:
$$y = \frac{10 - 3\left(\frac{16}{7}\right)}{2} = \frac{\frac{70-48}{7}}{2} = \frac{\frac{22}{7}}{2} = \frac{22}{14} = \frac{11}{7}$$

So the point of intersection is $\left(\dfrac{16}{7}, \dfrac{11}{7}\right)$.

**Check:** $5\left(\dfrac{16}{7}\right) - 6\left(\dfrac{11}{7}\right) = \dfrac{80}{7} - \dfrac{66}{7} = \dfrac{14}{7} = 2$ $\checkmark$; $3\left(\dfrac{16}{7}\right) + 2\left(\dfrac{11}{7}\right) = \dfrac{48}{7} + \dfrac{22}{7} = \dfrac{70}{7} = 10$ $\checkmark$

#### Step 2: Find the slope of the required line.

The line $4x - 7y + 13 = 0$ has slope:
$$m_1 = -\frac{4}{-7} = \frac{4}{7}$$

For a line perpendicular to it, $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$:
$$\frac{4}{7} \times m_2 = -1 \implies m_2 = -\frac{7}{4}$$

#### Step 3: Write the equation through $\left(\dfrac{16}{7}, \dfrac{11}{7}\right)$ with slope $-\dfrac{7}{4}$.

$$y - \frac{11}{7} = -\frac{7}{4}\left(x - \frac{16}{7}\right)$$

Multiply both sides by $28$ (LCM of $7$ and $4$):
$$28y - 44 = -49\left(x - \frac{16}{7}\right)$$
$$28y - 44 = -49x + 112$$
$$49x + 28y - 44 - 112 = 0$$
$$49x + 28y - 156 = 0$$

**Verification:** Substitute $\left(\dfrac{16}{7}, \dfrac{11}{7}\right)$:
$$49\left(\frac{16}{7}\right) + 28\left(\frac{11}{7}\right) - 156 = 112 + 44 - 156 = 0 \quad \checkmark$$

Also check perpendicularity: slope of $49x+28y-156=0$ is $-\dfrac{49}{28}=-\dfrac{7}{4}$; product with $\dfrac{4}{7}$ is $-1$. $\checkmark$

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{49x + 28y - 156 = 0}$$
