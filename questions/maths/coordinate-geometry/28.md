## Find the equation of the straight line passing through the point $(6, -2)$ whose sum of positive intercepts on the coordinate axes is $5$.

---

#### Let the $x$-intercept be $a$ and the $y$-intercept be $b$, with both positive and $a + b = 5$, so $b = 5 - a$.

Intercept form of a straight line:
$$\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} = 1 \implies \frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{5-a} = 1$$

Since the line passes through $(6, -2)$, substitute $x = 6$, $y = -2$:
$$\frac{6}{a} + \frac{-2}{5-a} = 1$$

Multiply throughout by $a(5-a)$:
$$6(5-a) - 2a = a(5-a)$$
$$30 - 6a - 2a = 5a - a^2$$
$$30 - 8a = 5a - a^2$$
$$a^2 - 8a - 5a + 30 = 0$$
$$a^2 - 13a + 30 = 0$$

Factorising:
$$(a - 10)(a - 3) = 0$$
$$a = 10 \quad \text{or} \quad a = 3$$

#### Choosing the root that satisfies "positive intercepts":

If $a = 10$: $b = 5 - 10 = -5$, which is **negative** — rejected since both intercepts must be positive.

If $a = 3$: $b = 5 - 3 = 2$, and both $a = 3 > 0$ and $b = 2 > 0$ — **accepted**.

#### Writing the equation with $a = 3$, $b = 2$:

$$\frac{x}{3} + \frac{y}{2} = 1$$

Multiplying throughout by $6$ (LCM of $3$ and $2$):
$$2x + 3y = 6$$
$$2x + 3y - 6 = 0$$

**Verification:** Substitute $(6, -2)$:
$$2(6) + 3(-2) - 6 = 12 - 6 - 6 = 0 \quad \checkmark$$
Intercepts: at $y=0$, $x=3$ (positive); at $x=0$, $y=2$ (positive); sum $=3+2=5$. $\checkmark$

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{2x + 3y - 6 = 0}$$
