## Let $A(3,-4)$, $B(9,-4)$, $C(5,-7)$ and $D(7,-7)$ be the points. Show that $ABCD$ is a trapezium.

---

#### Step 1: Recall the condition for a trapezium.

A quadrilateral is a **trapezium** if exactly **one pair** of opposite sides is parallel (and the other pair is not parallel). If both pairs were parallel, it would be a parallelogram instead.

#### Step 2: Find the slope of side $AB$.

$$A(3,-4), \quad B(9,-4)$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{-4 - (-4)}{9 - 3} = \frac{0}{6} = 0$$

So $AB$ is horizontal.

#### Step 3: Find the slope of side $CD$.

$$C(5,-7), \quad D(7,-7)$$
$$m_{CD} = \frac{-7 - (-7)}{7 - 5} = \frac{0}{2} = 0$$

So $CD$ is also horizontal.

Since $m_{AB} = m_{CD} = 0$, we have $AB \parallel CD$.

#### Step 4: Find the slope of side $BC$.

$$B(9,-4), \quad C(5,-7)$$
$$m_{BC} = \frac{-7 - (-4)}{5 - 9} = \frac{-3}{-4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

#### Step 5: Find the slope of side $AD$.

$$A(3,-4), \quad D(7,-7)$$
$$m_{AD} = \frac{-7 - (-4)}{7 - 3} = \frac{-3}{4}$$

Since $m_{BC} = \dfrac{3}{4}$ and $m_{AD} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$, we have $m_{BC} \ne m_{AD}$, so $BC$ is **not** parallel to $AD$.

#### Conclusion

Exactly one pair of opposite sides ($AB$ and $CD$) is parallel, while the other pair ($BC$ and $AD$) is not parallel. Therefore, by definition, $ABCD$ is a **trapezium**.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{ABCD \text{ is a trapezium, since } AB \parallel CD \text{ (slope } 0\text{) but } BC \nparallel AD}$$
