## If the vertices of a $\Delta ABC$ are $A(6,2)$, $B(-5,-1)$ and $C(1,9)$, find the equation of the median from $A$.

---

#### Step 1: Recall what a median is.

The median from vertex $A$ is the line segment joining $A$ to the **midpoint of the opposite side** $BC$.

#### Step 2: Find the midpoint $M$ of $BC$.

$$B(-5,-1), \quad C(1,9)$$
$$M = \left(\frac{-5+1}{2}, \frac{-1+9}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{-4}{2}, \frac{8}{2}\right) = (-2, 4)$$

#### Step 3: Find the slope of the line joining $A(6,2)$ and $M(-2,4)$.

$$m = \frac{4 - 2}{-2 - 6} = \frac{2}{-8} = -\frac{1}{4}$$

#### Step 4: Write the equation of the median using the point-slope form through $A(6,2)$.

$$y - 2 = -\frac{1}{4}(x - 6)$$

Multiply both sides by $4$:
$$4(y - 2) = -(x - 6)$$
$$4y - 8 = -x + 6$$
$$x + 4y - 8 - 6 = 0$$
$$x + 4y - 14 = 0$$

#### Verification

Substitute $A(6,2)$: $6 + 4(2) - 14 = 6 + 8 - 14 = 0$ $\checkmark$
Substitute $M(-2,4)$: $-2 + 4(4) - 14 = -2 + 16 - 14 = 0$ $\checkmark$

Both points lie on the line, confirming the equation.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{x + 4y - 14 = 0}$$
