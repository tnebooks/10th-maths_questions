## Given the four straight lines (i) $l_1: 3y = 4x + 5$ (ii) $l_2: 4y = 3x - 1$ (iii) $l_3: 4y + 3x = 7$ (iv) $l_4: 4x + 3y = 2$, which of the following statements is true?

---

#### Step 1: Write each line in slope-intercept form and find its slope.

$$l_1: 3y = 4x+5 \implies y = \frac{4}{3}x + \frac{5}{3}, \quad m_1 = \frac{4}{3}$$
$$l_2: 4y = 3x-1 \implies y = \frac{3}{4}x - \frac{1}{4}, \quad m_2 = \frac{3}{4}$$
$$l_3: 4y+3x=7 \implies y = -\frac{3}{4}x + \frac{7}{4}, \quad m_3 = -\frac{3}{4}$$
$$l_4: 4x+3y=2 \implies y = -\frac{4}{3}x + \frac{2}{3}, \quad m_4 = -\frac{4}{3}$$

#### Step 2: Check each option.

$l_1, l_2$: $m_1 \times m_2 = \frac{4}{3}\times\frac{3}{4} = 1 \ne -1$ — not perpendicular.
$l_1, l_4$: $m_1 = \frac{4}{3}$, $m_4 = -\frac{4}{3}$ — not equal, not parallel.
$l_2, l_3$: $m_2 = \frac{3}{4}$, $m_3 = -\frac{3}{4}$ — not equal, not parallel.
$l_2, l_4$: $m_2 \times m_4 = \frac{3}{4}\times\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = -1$ — **perpendicular**.

**Verification:** Product of slopes $= -1$ is the exact condition for perpendicularity, so $l_2$ and $l_4$ are perpendicular. $\checkmark$

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{l_2 \text{ and } l_4 \text{ are perpendicular}}$$
