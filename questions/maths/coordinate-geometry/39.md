## If the vertices of $\Delta ABC$ are $A(-3,0)$, $B(10,-2)$ and $C(11,2)$, find the equation of the median passing through vertex $B$.

---

#### Step 1: Recall what a median is.

The median through vertex $B$ is the line segment joining $B$ to the **midpoint of the opposite side** $AC$.

#### Step 2: Find the midpoint $M$ of $AC$.

$$A(-3,0), \quad C(11,2)$$
$$M = \left(\frac{-3+11}{2}, \frac{0+2}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{8}{2}, \frac{2}{2}\right) = (4, 1)$$

#### Step 3: Find the slope of the line joining $B(10,-2)$ and $M(4,1)$.

$$m = \frac{1 - (-2)}{4 - 10} = \frac{3}{-6} = -\frac{1}{2}$$

#### Step 4: Write the equation of the median using the point-slope form through $B(10,-2)$.

$$y - (-2) = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 10)$$
$$y + 2 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 10)$$

Multiply both sides by $2$:
$$2(y+2) = -(x-10)$$
$$2y + 4 = -x + 10$$
$$x + 2y + 4 - 10 = 0$$
$$x + 2y - 6 = 0$$

#### Verification

Substitute $B(10,-2)$: $10 + 2(-2) - 6 = 10 - 4 - 6 = 0$ $\checkmark$
Substitute $M(4,1)$: $4 + 2(1) - 6 = 4 + 2 - 6 = 0$ $\checkmark$

Both points lie on the line, confirming the equation.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{x + 2y - 6 = 0}$$
