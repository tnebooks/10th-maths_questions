## Find the equation of the straight line joining the point of intersection of $7x-3y=-12$ and $2y=x+3$, and the point of intersection of $3x+y+2=0$ and $x-2y-4=0$.

---

#### Step 1: Find the first point of intersection — of $7x - 3y = -12$ and $2y = x + 3$.

From $2y = x+3$: $x = 2y - 3$.

Substitute into $7x - 3y = -12$:
$$7(2y-3) - 3y = -12$$
$$14y - 21 - 3y = -12$$
$$11y = 9 \implies y = \frac{9}{11}$$
$$x = 2\left(\frac{9}{11}\right) - 3 = \frac{18}{11} - \frac{33}{11} = -\frac{15}{11}$$

So the first point is $P_1 = \left(-\dfrac{15}{11}, \dfrac{9}{11}\right)$.

#### Step 2: Find the second point of intersection — of $3x+y+2=0$ and $x-2y-4=0$.

From $x - 2y - 4 = 0$: $x = 2y + 4$.

Substitute into $3x+y+2=0$:
$$3(2y+4) + y + 2 = 0$$
$$6y + 12 + y + 2 = 0$$
$$7y = -14 \implies y = -2$$
$$x = 2(-2) + 4 = 0$$

So the second point is $P_2 = (0, -2)$.

#### Step 3: Find the slope of the line joining $P_1$ and $P_2$.

$$m = \frac{-2 - \frac{9}{11}}{0 - \left(-\frac{15}{11}\right)} = \frac{-\frac{22}{11} - \frac{9}{11}}{\frac{15}{11}} = \frac{-\frac{31}{11}}{\frac{15}{11}} = -\frac{31}{15}$$

#### Step 4: Write the equation using the point-slope form through $P_2(0,-2)$.

$$y - (-2) = -\frac{31}{15}(x - 0)$$
$$y + 2 = -\frac{31}{15}x$$

Multiply both sides by $15$:
$$15y + 30 = -31x$$
$$31x + 15y + 30 = 0$$

#### Verification

Substitute $P_2(0,-2)$: $31(0) + 15(-2) + 30 = -30 + 30 = 0$ $\checkmark$
Substitute $P_1\left(-\frac{15}{11}, \frac{9}{11}\right)$: $31\left(-\frac{15}{11}\right) + 15\left(\frac{9}{11}\right) + 30 = \frac{-465 + 135 + 330}{11} = \frac{0}{11} = 0$ $\checkmark$

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{31x + 15y + 30 = 0}$$
