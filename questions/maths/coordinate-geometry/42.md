## If the vertices of a triangle are $A(-3,0)$, $B(10,-2)$ and $C(12,3)$, find the equation of the altitude drawn from $A$.

---

#### Step 1: Recall what an altitude is.

The altitude from vertex $A$ is the line through $A$ that is **perpendicular** to the opposite side $BC$.

#### Step 2: Find the slope of side $BC$.

$$B(10,-2), \quad C(12,3)$$
$$m_{BC} = \frac{3 - (-2)}{12 - 10} = \frac{5}{2}$$

#### Step 3: Find the slope of the altitude (perpendicular to $BC$).

Since the altitude is perpendicular to $BC$:
$$m_{\text{altitude}} = -\frac{1}{m_{BC}} = -\frac{1}{\frac{5}{2}} = -\frac{2}{5}$$

#### Step 4: Write the equation of the altitude using the point-slope form through $A(-3,0)$.

$$y - 0 = -\frac{2}{5}(x - (-3))$$
$$y = -\frac{2}{5}(x + 3)$$

Multiply both sides by $5$:
$$5y = -2(x+3)$$
$$5y = -2x - 6$$
$$2x + 5y + 6 = 0$$

#### Verification

Substitute $A(-3,0)$: $2(-3) + 5(0) + 6 = -6 + 0 + 6 = 0$ $\checkmark$
Check perpendicularity: $m_{BC} \times m_{\text{altitude}} = \frac{5}{2} \times \left(-\frac{2}{5}\right) = -1$ $\checkmark$

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{2x + 5y + 6 = 0}$$
