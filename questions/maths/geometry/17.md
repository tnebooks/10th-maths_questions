## In the given figure, $\triangle PQR$ has $S$ on $PQ$ and $T$ on $PR$ with $ST \parallel QR$, $PS = 2\text{ cm}$ and $SQ = 3\text{ cm}$, then the ratio of the area of $\triangle PQR$ to the area of $\triangle PST$ is

---

#### Since $ST \parallel QR$, by AA similarity, $\triangle PST \sim \triangle PQR$.
From the figure:
$$PQ = PS + SQ = 2 + 3 = 5\text{ cm}$$
The ratio of corresponding sides:
$$\frac{PS}{PQ} = \frac{2}{5}$$
By the theorem on areas of similar triangles, the ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of corresponding sides:
$$\frac{\text{Area}(\triangle PST)}{\text{Area}(\triangle PQR)} = \left(\frac{PS}{PQ}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{25}$$
Therefore:
$$\frac{\text{Area}(\triangle PQR)}{\text{Area}(\triangle PST)} = \frac{25}{4}$$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{25:4}$
