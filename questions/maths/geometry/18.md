## If $\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{BC}{FD}$, then $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle EDF$ are similar when

---

#### By the SAS similarity criterion, two triangles are similar if two pairs of corresponding sides are in proportion and the angle included between those two sides in each triangle is equal.
In $\triangle ABC$, the sides $AB$ and $BC$ have the common vertex $B$ — so the angle included between them is $\angle B$.
In $\triangle EDF$, reading the ratio $\dfrac{BC}{FD}$ as corresponding to $DE$ and then $FD$, the sides $DE$ and $FD$ have the common vertex $D$ — so the angle included between them is $\angle D$.
For the given proportion $\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{BC}{FD}$ to make the triangles similar by SAS, the included angles must be equal:
$$\angle B = \angle D$$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{\angle B = \angle D}$
