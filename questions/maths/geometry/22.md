## State and prove Angle Bisector Theorem.

---

#### **Statement:**
The internal bisector of an angle of a triangle divides the opposite side internally in the ratio of the corresponding sides containing the angle.

#### **Proof:**

**Given:** In $\triangle ABC$, $AD$ is the internal bisector of $\angle A$.

**To prove:** $\dfrac{AB}{AC} = \dfrac{BD}{CD}$

**Construction:** Draw a line through $C$ parallel to $AB$. Extend $AD$ to meet this line at $E$.

| No. | Statement | Reason |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | $\angle 1 = \angle 1$ ($\angle BAD = \angle CED$) | Alternate interior angles, since $AB \parallel CE$ |
| 2 | $\angle 2 = \angle 2$ ($\angle DAC = \angle ACE$) | $\angle 1 = \angle 2$ ($AD$ is angle bisector) $\implies \angle CED = \angle ACE$ |
| 3 | $\triangle ACE$ is isosceles | In $\triangle ACE$, $\angle CAE = \angle CEA \implies AC = CE$ |
| 4 | $\triangle ABD \sim \triangle ECD$ | By AA similarity |
| 5 | $\dfrac{AB}{CE} = \dfrac{BD}{CD}$ | Corresponding sides are proportional |
| 6 | $\dfrac{AB}{AC} = \dfrac{BD}{CD}$ | Since $CE = AC$ (from step 3) |

Hence, the theorem is proved.
