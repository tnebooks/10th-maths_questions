## If the sum of three consecutive terms of an Arithmetic Progression is $27$ and their product is $288$, find the three terms.

---

#### Let the three consecutive terms of the A.P. be written symmetrically around the middle term as $(a-d)$, $a$, $(a+d)$, where $a$ is the middle term and $d$ is the common difference.

**Step 1: Use the sum condition**

$$(a-d) + a + (a+d) = 27$$
$$3a = 27$$
$$a = 9$$

**Step 2: Use the product condition**

$$(a-d) \times a \times (a+d) = 288$$
$$a(a^2 - d^2) = 288$$

Substitute $a = 9$:
$$9(81 - d^2) = 288$$
$$81 - d^2 = 32$$
$$d^2 = 81 - 32 = 49$$
$$d = \pm 7$$

**Step 3: Find the three terms**

If $d = 7$: terms are $a-d = 2$, $a = 9$, $a+d = 16$, i.e., $2, 9, 16$.

If $d = -7$: terms are $a-d = 16$, $a = 9$, $a+d = 2$, i.e., $16, 9, 2$ (the same three numbers in reverse order).

**Verification:** Sum $= 2+9+16 = 27$ ✓. Product $= 2 \times 9 \times 16 = 288$ ✓.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{\text{The three terms are } 2, 9, 16}$$
