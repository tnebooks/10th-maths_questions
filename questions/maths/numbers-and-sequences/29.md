## $S_1$, $S_2$, and $S_3$ are respectively the sums of the first $n$, $2n$ and $3n$ terms of an Arithmetic Progression. Prove that $S_3 = 3(S_2 - S_1)$.

---

#### Let the Arithmetic Progression have first term $a$ and common difference $d$. Use the sum formula $S_k = \dfrac{k}{2}\big[2a + (k-1)d\big]$ for the sum of the first $k$ terms.

**Step 1: Write $S_1$, $S_2$, $S_3$**

$$S_1 = \frac{n}{2}\big[2a + (n-1)d\big]$$

$$S_2 = \frac{2n}{2}\big[2a + (2n-1)d\big] = n\big[2a + (2n-1)d\big]$$

$$S_3 = \frac{3n}{2}\big[2a + (3n-1)d\big]$$

**Step 2: Compute $S_2 - S_1$**

$$S_2 - S_1 = n\big[2a + (2n-1)d\big] - \frac{n}{2}\big[2a + (n-1)d\big]$$

Take $\dfrac{n}{2}$ common:

$$S_2 - S_1 = \frac{n}{2}\Big\{2\big[2a + (2n-1)d\big] - \big[2a + (n-1)d\big]\Big\}$$

$$= \frac{n}{2}\Big\{4a + 2(2n-1)d - 2a - (n-1)d\Big\}$$

$$= \frac{n}{2}\Big\{2a + \big[2(2n-1) - (n-1)\big]d\Big\}$$

$$= \frac{n}{2}\Big\{2a + \big[4n - 2 - n + 1\big]d\Big\}$$

$$= \frac{n}{2}\big[2a + (3n-1)d\big]$$

**Step 3: Multiply by $3$ and compare with $S_3$**

$$3(S_2 - S_1) = 3 \times \frac{n}{2}\big[2a + (3n-1)d\big] = \frac{3n}{2}\big[2a + (3n-1)d\big]$$

This is exactly $S_3$ from Step 1.

**Verification (numerical check with $a=2$, $d=3$, $n=4$):** $S_1 = S_4 = 26$, $S_2 = S_8 = 100$, $S_3 = S_{12} = 222$. Check: $3(S_2-S_1) = 3(100-26) = 3(74) = 222 = S_3$. ✓

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{S_3 = 3(S_2 - S_1) \text{ — proved}}$$
