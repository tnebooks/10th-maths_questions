## Given $A = \{1,2\}$, $B = \{1,2,3,4\}$, $C = \{5,6\}$ and $D = \{5,6,7,8\}$, which of the following statements is correct?

---

#### We check which subset relation among the given Cartesian products is true. Since $A = \{1,2\} \subset \{1,2,3,4\} = B$ and $C = \{5,6\} \subset \{5,6,7,8\} = D$, every ordered pair $(a,c)$ with $a \in A, c \in C$ also satisfies $a \in B$ and $c \in D$. Hence every element of $A \times C$ is an element of $B \times D$, which gives $(A \times C) \subset (B \times D)$. The reverse inclusion $(B \times D) \subset (A \times C)$ is false since, for example, $(3,5) \in B \times D$ but $3 \notin A$. Checking the remaining options: $(A \times B) \subset (A \times D)$ fails because elements like $3, 4 \in B$ are not in $D$, and $(D \times A) \subset (B \times A)$ would require $D \subset B$, which is false since $5,6,7,8 \notin B$. Therefore the correct statement is $(A \times C) \subset (B \times D)$.
