## The temperatures of two cities A and B during winter are given below:
## City A: $18^\circ\text{C}, 20^\circ\text{C}, 22^\circ\text{C}, 24^\circ\text{C}, 26^\circ\text{C}$
## City B: $11^\circ\text{C}, 14^\circ\text{C}, 15^\circ\text{C}, 17^\circ\text{C}, 18^\circ\text{C}$
## Which city is more consistent in temperature?

---

#### The consistency of a data set is measured by its coefficient of variation ($C.V.$). The data set with the smaller coefficient of variation is said to be more consistent:
$$C.V. = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{x}} \times 100\%$$

**Step 1: City A calculations**

Observations: $18, 20, 22, 24, 26$ ($n = 5$).
- Mean: $\bar{x}_A = \frac{18 + 20 + 22 + 24 + 26}{5} = \frac{110}{5} = 22^\circ\text{C}$

Deviations $d = x - 22$:
- $18 - 22 = -4 \implies d^2 = 16$
- $20 - 22 = -2 \implies d^2 = 4$
- $22 - 22 = 0 \implies d^2 = 0$
- $24 - 22 = 2 \implies d^2 = 4$
- $26 - 22 = 4 \implies d^2 = 16$
- $\sum d^2 = 16 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 40$

Standard deviation:
$$\sigma_A = \sqrt{\frac{\sum d^2}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{40}{5}} = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.83$$

Coefficient of variation:
$$C.V._A = \frac{\sigma_A}{\bar{x}_A} \times 100 = \frac{2.83}{22} \times 100 \approx 12.86\%$$

**Step 2: City B calculations**

Observations: $11, 14, 15, 17, 18$ ($n = 5$).
- Mean: $\bar{x}_B = \frac{11 + 14 + 15 + 17 + 18}{5} = \frac{75}{5} = 15^\circ\text{C}$

Deviations $d = x - 15$:
- $11 - 15 = -4 \implies d^2 = 16$
- $14 - 15 = -1 \implies d^2 = 1$
- $15 - 15 = 0 \implies d^2 = 0$
- $17 - 15 = 2 \implies d^2 = 4$
- $18 - 15 = 3 \implies d^2 = 9$
- $\sum d^2 = 16 + 1 + 0 + 4 + 9 = 30$

Standard deviation:
$$\sigma_B = \sqrt{\frac{\sum d^2}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{30}{5}} = \sqrt{6} \approx 2.45$$

Coefficient of variation:
$$C.V._B = \frac{\sigma_B}{\bar{x}_B} \times 100 = \frac{2.45}{15} \times 100 \approx 16.33\%$$

**Step 3: Comparison**
$$C.V._A (12.86\%) < C.V._B (16.33\%)$$

Since City A has a lower coefficient of variation than City B, City A is more consistent in temperature.

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{City\ A\text{ is more consistent}}$$
