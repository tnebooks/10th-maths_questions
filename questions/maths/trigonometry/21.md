## The value of $\sin^2\theta + \dfrac{1}{1+\tan^2\theta}$ is equal to

---

#### By the Pythagorean identity, $1+\tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$.

So:
$$\frac{1}{1+\tan^2\theta} = \frac{1}{\sec^2\theta} = \cos^2\theta$$

Substituting back into the expression:
$$\sin^2\theta + \frac{1}{1+\tan^2\theta} = \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta$$

By the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$:
$$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$$

**Verification (with $\theta=30^\circ$):** $\sin^2 30^\circ = \frac14$, $\tan^2 30^\circ = \frac13$, so $\frac{1}{1+\frac13} = \frac{1}{\frac43} = \frac34$. Sum $= \frac14+\frac34 = 1$. $\checkmark$

**Answer:**
$$\mathbf{1}$$
